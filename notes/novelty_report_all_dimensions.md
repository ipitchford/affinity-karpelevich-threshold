# Bounded novelty-gate rerun: all-dimensional affinity-containment failure

Search date: 20 October 2026. Contribution unit: mathematical statement and complete proof of failure for every n>=4. The known boundary realisations and the known resetting spectral shift are not claimed as new objects or results.

## Frozen input and fingerprints

`extension_target.yaml` records quantifiers, normalisation, scope and exclusions. `extension_fingerprints.json`, produced by `python3 extension_fingerprint.py`, supplies exact n=4,5,6 matrices at t=1/2 and epsilon=1/1000; characteristic polynomials, rank, trace, determinant, row sums, sorted entries, zero patterns, and canonical hashes under transpose and simultaneous relabelling are included. Those finite parameters are object-identification instances rather than independently certified counterexamples.

The low-complexity normal forms are `(z^2-t)^m-(1-t)^m*z` and `z*(z^2-t)^m-(1-t)^m`. The graph interpretation is a chain of two-cycles closed by a long cycle; the resetting interpretation is rank-one stationary teleportation. The asymptotic affinity is the maximum mean of an antisymmetric edge-valuation matrix. These aliases connect nonnegative inverse eigenvalue problems, stochastic thermodynamics, Markov-chain resetting, and max-plus cycle means.

## Primary sources inspected and verified translations

1. Johnson and Paparella (2017), https://doi.org/10.1016/j.laa.2017.01.009, full PDF https://arxiv.org/pdf/1611.06970v2, Theorems 2.1 and 3.2. The even matrix is exactly their Type II construction with q=2,s=n-1; the odd matrix is exactly Type III with q=2,s=n,d=1. Their stochastic-matrix convention agrees with ours. This is an object-identity collision, and both the formulas and matrices are explicitly attributed.
2. Kirkland and Šmigoc (2022), https://doi.org/10.1016/j.laa.2021.11.016, full PDF https://arxiv.org/pdf/2110.01040. The introduction and structural scope confirm prior classification of sparse Type II/III realisations. No `affinity` occurrence in the inspected text. This is further prior art for boundary-matrix structure, not a refutation of the thermodynamic affine claim.
3. Rose et al. (2018), https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevE.98.022129, full PDF https://arxiv.org/pdf/1806.01298, previously inspected in the four-state gate. Rank-one resetting uniformly shifts nonzero eigenvalues. This ingredient is known and attributed.
4. Xu et al. (2025), https://doi.org/10.1103/z4t2-18cx, https://arxiv.org/html/2507.08938v2, reread on 20 October. The precise claim uses supplementary beta-prime, the maximum mean cycle affinity, not the main-paper stationary-edge beta. This is the statement being refuted.
5. Kolchinsky, Ohga and Ito (2026), https://arxiv.org/html/2605.15884v3, version 3 dated 8 October 2026. Theorem 5 gives the affine image of the convex hull of Farey roots, not the curved Karpelevich region. The exact localisation statement and discussion were reread. The source's other multicyclic counterexample concerns winding-index correspondence, not our target.
6. Chruściński, Denisov, Tarnowski and Życzkowski (2025), https://arxiv.org/html/2504.07903v1. Title, abstract, introductory definitions and affinity full-text search inspected. This concerns a different generator normalisation and modified Karpelevich regions without the cycle-affinity parameter. No `affinity` occurrence was found. It does not furnish the thermodynamic containment statement.
